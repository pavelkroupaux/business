Coinmate · 2025–2026 · Regulated crypto exchange

A rebrand with two constraints pulling against each other

What it proves Judgement when two constraints collide. Holding a position against someone senior.

  1. StuckTwo constraints collide
  2. MoveMap the competition that does not exist yet
  3. MoveTokens before iteration
  4. MoveTest where the pairing breaks
  5. MoveArgue with their own brand values
  6. ResultOur palette shipped

What could not be decided

One colour was effectively a global competitor’s. The new identity had to move away from it without colliding with domestic competitors, or with banks preparing to enter crypto that had no colours yet.

What I did

I treated an empty stretch of the market as occupied, built the token mechanism before iterating, and argued the palette on the brand values the external consultant had written himself.

How it ended

Our combination shipped. Management then moved the identity decisions to me.

Read the whole case

What I was risking

Pick a colour a large bank adopts two years from now and the rebrand gets done again, this time with the identity already deployed across the marketing site, the mobile app and the web application.

The moves

  • Mapped the competition that did not exist yet. Research into which banks were signalling a move turned an open question into a bounded space. It stopped being a conversation about taste.
  • Built the mechanism before iterating. Figma mapped to tokens, so a colour change propagated in minutes instead of days. Without it we would have produced one concept, not five.
  • Tested where the pairing was most likely to break. Typified screens where the contrast between primary and secondary was harshest, with the colleagues furthest from design rather than product and engineering.
  • Held the line using the other side’s own criteria. An external brand consultant brought in a designer of his own. I argued against the result on substance: the proposed palette worked against the brand values the same consultant had authored.

What it cost

Three rounds of iteration instead of the deeper testing that was planned. An external agency ran the key visual past the target audience and I supplied distilled variants, not final creative. Two months at two days a week, alongside front-end delivery.

The identity has not shipped publicly yet. Nothing here shows it. Assets follow once the rebrand is live.

Coinmate · 2025 · Quick trade, before and after funnel analysis

The claim I retired myself

What it proves Retiring a claim before anyone else has to.

  1. StuckSelling is hard to find
  2. MoveHypothesis: make it visible
  3. MoveMeasure the whole funnel
  4. MoveControl for the market
  5. MoveRetire the claim, say it first
  6. ResultThe barrier is outside the interface

What could not be decided

Most customers used the quick trade calculator and selling was hard to find in it. I believed visibility was the barrier.

What I did

I measured the whole funnel, not only the step I was proud of, and controlled for market conditions. Then I retired my own claim and said it first.

How it ended

Discoverability rose and held. Selling did not. The diagnosis was handed over, not closed.

Read the whole case

What I was risking

If the number I had moved was not the one that mattered, I would be handing my own stakeholders evidence against my own work, in a company where I was the design function and my budget depended on it. Reporting the rise in discoverability and moving on was available, defensible and true.

The moves

  • Replaced the switch with two explicit actions. Selling became visible as its own action above the calculator.
  • Measured the whole funnel. Discoverability of selling rose and held under significance testing. Completion further down the flow still dropped and the share of sell orders stayed flat.
  • Retired the claim rather than restating it softer. The change stayed, because it earned its place on discoverability, which is a different justification from the one I started with.

What it cost

I did not get to fix it. People could find selling and still did not sell, which puts the barrier outside the interface: fees, tax treatment, or simply that people who buy crypto are not looking to sell it.

The figures sit with their baselines. I bring them to the call rather than the web page.

Heirloom · 2026 · Digital legacy platform, zero to one

They asked for mockups. I built a machine.

What it proves AI as infrastructure a team works through.

  1. StuckExpected: mockups
  2. MovePrototypes on the production repository
  3. MoveOne live address per screen
  4. MoveComments on the screen itself
  5. MoveAI first pass, every pass reviewed
  6. ResultArguments moved before the build

What could not be decided

The team needed a whole pivot specified and what was expected from me was mockups. But mockups were the thing slowing us down: nothing could be judged until it was built.

What I did

I did not start specifying. I built the pipeline: prototypes assembled from the real components, one live address per screen, and comments on the screen itself.

How it ended

The arguments that used to happen after a build started happening before one.

Read the whole case

What I was risking

Weeks with no visible output at a startup where runway was the clock. If the pipeline had not worked, iteration time would have grown, development would have run long, and the runway before the next investor conversation would have shortened.

The moves

  • Connected prototyping to the production repository. Screens assembled from the real components and tokens rather than drawn to look like them.
  • One live address per screen. Review stopped being a meeting and became a link.
  • Built a commenting tool inside the prototype. Feedback had been arriving in three places at once and getting lost between them.
  • Used AI for the first pass, reviewed every pass. The model is fast at producing the version that already exists and poor at producing the version that should.
  • Had the output peer-reviewed by a software engineer. That closed the gap between what a stakeholder describes and what an engineer can implement.

What it cost

The prototypes are static and run on mock data. The company did not find product-market fit and pivoted. That is a separate question from whether these were good decisions.

Leeaf · 2021–2023 · Fertility healthcare: iOS, Android, physician portal

An abstraction built before the thing it abstracts

What it proves Design systems. Leading through a system instead of through approvals.

  1. StuckWhite-label by copying files
  2. MoveClinics as configuration, not copies
  3. MoveCommit before the flows settle
  4. MoveDesigners work without me
  5. ResultOther clinics ran on it

What could not be decided

The product had to run white-labelled for other IVF clinics and the planned path was to duplicate the design files per clinic. That works until the third one.

What I did

I made the difference between clinics a configuration, committed before the flows had settled, and built it so the two designers could produce variants without my approval.

How it ended

The white-label version is what other IVF clinics ran on. The people using it did not need me.

“sometimes adjusting designs based on technical challenges”Petr TomíčekiOS Architect, Jablotron Cloud ServicesWorked together at Leeaf · 2023
Read the whole case

What was expected of me instead

Screens. The company was young, the product was not built, and a system for clinics we did not have yet looked like a designer optimising for his own comfort.

What I was risking

An abstraction over a product structure that did not exist yet. If the flows had moved underneath it, a rebuild under load, on a healthcare product, with clinics already running on it.

The moves

  • Made the difference between clinics a configuration. A new clinic became a set of values instead of a new set of files.
  • Committed before the flows settled, deliberately. Waiting for the final structure would have meant retrofitting the system onto finished screens, which in practice means never doing it.
  • Built it so the designers could work without me. Two designers produced white-label variants without my supervision. The rules were in the system.
  • Co-created the IVF guide with clinicians. The content had to be correct and readable by someone frightened.

What it cost

The bet was taken on incomplete information. It held, but a different product direction in that period would have cost a rebuild, and I made the call knowing that.

  • ·Teams under a real constraint. A regulator, a licence, a clinical claim, somebody's savings.
  • ·Founders who will sit in the room instead of sending a delegate.

And who it does not

  • ×Places where problems get manufactured so somebody has something to solve.
  • ×Places where performance is the only value and people are a rounding error.

Rule this out early

What I am not

  • ×Not a metric-owning product manager. I close decisions. I do not carry your growth number.
  • ×Not an agency. There is no team behind me writing the deck.
  • ×Not a researcher. I use research that exists. I do not run a programme to postpone a decision.
  • ×Not a pair of hands in Figma. I direct visual craft. A designer owns the pixels.
  • ×Not available for equity instead of a fee.
